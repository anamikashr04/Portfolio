Overview

AI-Assisted Motion Tracking for Wrestling Training

This research explores self-training practices and posture-related injury risks among amateur wrestlers practicing within traditional akhāṛā environments. The project investigates how AI-assisted motion tracking can support safer training through real-time corrective feedback.

History

The evolution of Indian wrestling · History of Kushti

Indian wrestling, or kushti, originated from the ancient practice of malla-yuddha and evolved through Persian influences into the akhāṛā-based pehlwani tradition. Rooted in discipline, hierarchy, and embodied learning, wrestling later transitioned into modern competitive formats, contributing to India’s international sporting identity while continuing to preserve its traditional cultural ethos.

Over time, it transitioned from mud-pit community-centered training rooted in discipline and collective identity to mat-based competitive wrestling focused on performance, scientific training, and international competition, while still retaining elements of its traditional cultural ethos.

Problem

Problem Statement

Wrestling is a high-intensity combat sport associated with repetitive biomechanical stress and high injury prevalence, particularly affecting the knee, shoulder, and soft tissues. Injuries often emerge gradually due to fatigue, posture misalignment, and repetitive technical errors during training.

Within traditional wrestling systems, corrective feedback is largely dependent on coach observation. During unsupervised practice, technical deviations frequently remain unnoticed, increasing long-term injury risk.

Qualitative research

Qualitative Research Overview

An exploratory ethnographic study was conducted across traditional akhāṛā and wrestling training environments to understand behavioural patterns, self-training practices, posture awareness, and injury-related challenges among amateur wrestlers.

The qualitative phase incorporated Practice-as-Research (PaR), observation, shadowing, contextual inquiry, and auto-ethnographic reflection to examine embodied training experiences within wrestling communities.

Research methods
Practice-as-Research (PaR), Observation, Shadowing, Contextual Inquiry, Auto-Ethnographic Reflection
Sample method
Purposive Sampling (24 amateur wrestlers, and coaches)
Key observation
Independent self-practice and repetitive technical rehearsal emerged as recurring behaviours, particularly when coaches were unavailable.

Quantitative research

Research Objective and Aim of the Study

Research objective

  • What is the prevalence of injuries sustained during unsupervised self-practice among amateur wrestlers?
  • How do posture confidence and feedback availability relate to injury occurrence?
  • To what extent do technology-related beliefs predict willingness to adopt an AI-based training application?
  • What socio-cultural barriers within the akhāṛā system inhibit injury communication?
  • How can a technology-assisted system provide real-time corrective feedback while remaining culturally non-intrusive?

Aim of the study

To quantitatively investigate the relationship between self-training practices, posture-related injury risks, and the acceptance of AI-assisted corrective feedback systems among amateur Indian wrestlers.

Quantitative research

Research Methodology

A mixed-method, cross-sectional research approach was adopted to quantitatively validate behavioural insights identified during the qualitative phase.

  • Methodology

    • Survey-based quantitative study
    • Mixed-method triangulation

  • Sample

    • Purposive Sampling
    • 78 survey participants
    • 20 qualitative interviews

Quantitative research

Research Questions and Hypotheses

Research questions

  • How does self-training influence injury risk?
  • How does limited corrective feedback affect posture awareness?
  • How receptive are wrestlers toward AI-assisted training systems?

Hypotheses

  • H₀₁Unsupervised self-training does not have a significant impact on injury risk among amateur wrestlers.
  • H₁₁Unsupervised self-training significantly increases injury risk due to the absence of immediate corrective feedback.
  • H₀₂Wrestlers’ willingness to adopt AI-based training technology is not significantly influenced by its perceived usefulness for injury prevention.
  • H₁₂Wrestlers are more willing to adopt AI-based training technology when they perceive it as useful for injury prevention.

Risk factors

  • Injuries occurred most commonly during matches and self-practice sessions.
  • Fatigue, wrong posture, and lack of guidance emerged as the major causes of injuries.
  • Knees, shoulders, wrists, and neck were among the most commonly affected body areas.

Technology acceptance

  • Participants showed positive attitudes toward AI-assisted training systems.
  • Most respondents believed AI could improve posture and help reduce injuries.

Reflection

Limitations & Future Scope

Limitations

  • Relatively limited sample size
  • Testing focused only on selected foundational wrestling postures
  • Full spectrum of wrestling movements and variations not covered
  • Limited scope for comprehensive biomechanical analysis
  • Cultural and contextual constraints within traditional wrestling communities
  • Challenges in capturing diverse movement patterns within a single study

Future scope

  • Scalability of the system to other sports involving independent and repetitive self-training practices
  • Development of sport-specific AI movement and posture analysis models
  • Integration of advanced biomechanical analysis for injury-risk prediction
  • Creation of personalized training feedback ecosystems for grassroots and semi-professional athletes
  • Longitudinal studies to evaluate effectiveness across multiple sports and training environments

Conclusion

Indian Wrestling · Conclusions

This research explored self-training practices, posture awareness, and injury risks among amateur wrestlers through qualitative and quantitative research methods (n = 78). The findings identified limited corrective feedback during independent practice as a major challenge contributing to posture-related injuries.

The developed AI-assisted motion tracking prototype demonstrates the potential of real-time posture feedback to support safer and more informed self-training within traditional wrestling environments.
