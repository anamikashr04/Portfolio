Adaptive Museum Companion

A Service Design Approach to an Eye-Gaze Tracking App for Adaptive Art Mediation in Museums

Scroll to read the whole project. Select any image to zoom in.

Project Details

This project explores how museums can create meaningful, inclusive and conversation-driven experiences for visitors, with a focus on young adults.

DOMAIN
Museum Experience Design · Service Design
FOCUS AREA
Young Adult Visitors (18 - 30 years)
METHODS
Field Visits, Observations, Interviews, Shadowing, Secondary Research
OBJECTIVE
To understand young adults' behaviours, needs, and barriers across the museum journey to enhance engagement and meaningful experiences.
OUTCOME
Insights, journey maps and opportunity areas that inform the design of future museum experiences.

SECONDARY RESEARCH Study Research Focus Key Findings Design Implication for This Project Pine & Gilmore (1999)The Experience Economy Designing memorable experiences beyond products and services Organizations create greater value when they stage memorable, engaging experiences rather than simply delivering information or services. Museum interpretation should move beyond static labels to create immersive, memorable, and emotionally engaging visitor experiences. Falk & Dierking (2013)The Museum Experience Revisited Understanding how visitors learn and make meaning in museums Museum experiences are shaped by the interaction of personal, sociocultural, and physical contexts, making every visitor's experience unique. Interpretation should adapt to individual visitors' interests, prior knowledge, and museum context instead of providing one-size-fits-all information. Simon (2010)The Participatory Museum Visitor participation and co-creation in museums Visitors are more engaged when they actively participate, explore, contribute, and converse rather than passively consume information. AI should encourage curiosity, dialogue, and exploration, enabling visitors to become active participants in meaning-making. UNESCO (2025)Museums Around the World in the Face of AI Opportunities and challenges of AI adoption in museums AI can improve personalization, accessibility, multilingual interpretation, and visitor engagement while complementing—not replacing—museum professionals. AI should function as an adaptive interpretive layer that enhances accessibility and personalization while preserving human-centred museum experiences. May et al. (2009)Mobile Eye-Tracking for the Examination of Learning Experiences in Museums Understanding visitor attention through mobile eye tracking Mobile eye tracking unobtrusively captures visitors' natural visual attention and provides objective insights into exhibit engagement during real museum visits. Real-time gaze behaviour can be used as contextual input to personalize interpretation according to what visitors are actually observing.

John Falk’s Museum Visitor Identity Model (2009)

Text on this image, in the order it is stored in the file:

JOHN FALK'S MUSEUM VISITOR IDENTITY MODEL (2009) Explorers Facilitators Experience Seekers Professionals/ Hobbyists Rechargers Driven by their personal curiosity, their urge to discover new things. Visit the museum on behalf of others’ special interests in the exhibition or the subject-matter of the museum. Desire to see and experience a place, such as tourists. Visitors with specific knowledge in the subject matter of an exhibition and specific goals in mind. Rechargers seek a contemplative or restorative experience, often to let some steam out of their systems. Source: Falk, J. H. (2009). Identity and the museum visitor experience. Left Coast Press.

Problem Statement

Text on this image, in the order it is stored in the file:

PROBLEM STATEMENT Young adult visitors often struggle to move beyond passive observation, as existing museum interpretation relies heavily on static labels that provide limited contextual, personalized and conversational support for understanding works. As a result, opportunities for curiosity, meaningful engagement and personal connection with art remain underutilized throughout the museum journey.
